SCC · National Binding

R. v. Godoy

[1999] 1 S.C.R. 311 • 911 Distress Entry

CanLII Link Included

Operational Rule

An officer MAY force entry to locate a 911 caller and check life and safety, including after a disconnected call. An officer CANNOT use that entry as a general search for evidence; once the emergency is dealt with, another authority is required.

Legal Threshold

Waterfield: conduct falls within the duty to protect life, and forced entry is not an unjustifiable use of the powers associated with that duty. The scope of the entry remains safety-limited.

Charter & Cautions

Right to Counsel & Cautions

Field reference cards for arrest or detention (use your service card)

1. Reason & Right to Counsel (s. 10(a) & 10(b))

"You are under arrest for [plain-language offence]. You have the right to retain and instruct counsel without delay. You have the right to telephone any lawyer you wish. You also have the right to free advice from a legal aid lawyer. Do you understand? Do you want to call a lawyer?"

2. Primary Caution (Right to Silence)

"You are under arrest for [offence]. You do not have to say anything unless you wish to do so. Anything you do say may be used as evidence. Do you understand?"

3. Secondary Caution (Prior Officer / Break)

"Another officer has spoken to you / there has been a break since you last spoke to police. You do not have to say anything because of what the other officer said to you, or because of any promise, threat, or inducement. You are not obliged to say anything, but anything you do say may be given in evidence. Do you understand?"
